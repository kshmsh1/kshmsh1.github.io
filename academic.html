Academic research

Interdisciplinary work in financial-market structure, regulation, real analysis, and stochastic calculus at the University of Pennsylvania.

May 1, 2025 · Honors thesis · Finance and Statistics

A Comparative Analysis of Derivatives Markets in India and the European Union: Regulatory Frameworks and Market Structures

Institution: University of Pennsylvania · Huntsman Program

Advisors: Winston Dou, PhD (Department of Finance) and Jeffrey Babin (School of Engineering & Applied Science).

Research focus

My honors thesis examined derivatives markets in India and the European Union through the lenses of regulatory frameworks and market structure. The comparative approach considered how different institutional and supervisory arrangements shape market access, trading and risk-management practices, and market development.

Language and interdisciplinary work

Completed through Penn’s Huntsman Program in both English and French. The work combined finance, regulation, and comparative international-market analysis, reflecting my academic background in statistics and finance and my fluency in French.

This is an overview of the thesis topic, not a substitute for the full manuscript. No empirical findings, regulatory recommendations, or quantitative results are asserted here without the underlying thesis text.

Apr 30, 2025 · Predoctoral Directed Reading Program · Mathematics

Real Analysis and Itô Integration for Continuous-Time Models in Stochastic Calculus

Institution: University of Pennsylvania · Department of Mathematics · Directed Reading Program

Mentor: Keyuan Huang, PhD (Department of Mathematics).

Research focus

Over the semester, I explored the real-analysis foundations of stochastic calculus and how they support rigorous arguments about continuous-time processes. Topics included measure theory, convergence, function spaces, and Lebesgue integration, along with their applications to martingales, Brownian motion, and continuous-time models in mathematical finance.

Mathematical foundations

The study connected analytical tools and inequalities to the problems of bounding expectations, establishing convergence, and analyzing stochastic behavior. The emphasis was on understanding the assumptions and reasoning behind theoretical results, rather than treating stochastic models only as computational formulas.

Reading

  • J. Michael Steele, Stochastic Calculus and Financial Applications.
  • Steven Shreve, Stochastic Calculus for Finance II: Continuous-Time Models.
